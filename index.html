WALK, DON'T WATCH

Choose your own point of view.

Click the scene, then use W / A / S / D to move freely. Hold and drag the mouse to look around, or use the arrow keys to turn. Hold Shift to walk slowly and press R to return to the entrance. There is no automatic route. Approach a shop door, press E, wait for it to open fully, and walk inside. Step clear before closing a door. The café is under the yellow awning: look for the “Café · Entrance” sign on the first door at the near end of the terrace. From the initial street view, the café is behind you; press R to return to the terrace entrance if needed.

At the café easel, press E to choose a guest’s commission. The bookshop, fabric shop, and pottery studio offer three distinct activities: piece together four street fragments, drag color dots into three fabric bands, and draw through points of light. Press E near a counter to open its workstation. A live preview appears on the right. Save to the journal to keep your changes; leaving discards the current edits. Return to the café to deliver your work, read the guest’s descriptive feedback, and add the painting to your gallery. Press J for the journal and directions; it does not teleport you. Movement pauses while a panel is open. Use Tab / Enter to select and Esc to close.

“Enable mouse look” requests pointer lock so you can look around without holding a mouse button. Press Esc to exit. If your browser does not support it, you can still hold and drag.

Controller: left stick to move, right stick to look; X to interact, A for warm lights, Y to return, and Start for the journal. In a workstation, LB/RB changes tools; the D-pad adjusts the canvas or sliders; A acts or starts/ends a stroke; B closes. In the journal, use the D-pad to change focus and A to confirm. Physical-controller validation is still pending.

M2 / 8GB is a design target, not a performance guarantee. Balanced mode limits render resolution and shadow size; choose Performance mode if needed. Added shops are creative interpretations, not verified businesses from 1888. Chair orientation, tabletop placement, and shop-stock collision have been revised. This is a procedural scene prototype, not a Unity / Unreal Engine project or a historical reconstruction. There is no audio.
